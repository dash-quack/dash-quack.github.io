The Birth of Our Game

Theme research, initial ideation, Game Concept Card, the 7 Structural Elements of e-Games, and visual reference evidence for Ready, Frame, Shoot!.

1.1 Group Formation & Theme Confirmation

Assigned Theme: Educational

During the Week 1's practical session, we formed our group comprising 3 members. We confirmed our assigned theme: Educational.

We chose Educational because we wanted to teach the basics of photography.

Traditionally, grasping the exposure triangle (aperture, shutter speed, ISO) requires studying technical math or making mistakes in the wild. A game transforms those barriers and turns them into its mechanics: players can instantly tweak camera settings and see how opening an aperture creates dreamy background bokeh, or how adjusting shutter speed freezes high-speed action. Instead of just passing a quiz, players walk away with a personal gallery of unique digital photos that proves they've learned how to see the world like a photographer. The goal is to help the player turn these in-game skills into actual photography skills they can apply in real life.

Core Educational Foundations

1. The Exposure Triangle

Understanding how ISO, Aperture, and Shutter Speed interact to manage light intake and visual effects (brightness, depth of field, motion blur, and digital noise).

High-Concept Statement

An educational 3D game designed to teach real-world photography composition techniques and the exposure triangle (ISO, Aperture, Shutter Speed) through hands-on camera simulation, outdoor landscape, NPC quests, and an automated photo grading system.

Core Gameplay Features

  1. Camera View Adjustment: Freely adjust the lighting and optical settings (ISO, Aperture, Shutter Speed) via camera controls to snap photos under target conditions.
  2. Outdoor Scenery Documentation: Navigate stunning terrain to photograph and document complete quest objectives.
  3. NPC Quests: Talk to NPCs around the map offering objectives.
  4. Scoring & Photo Grading System: Objective photos are graded based on player position, camera settings, angle, framing, timing, and distance.

1.4 Application of Topic 1: The 7 Structural Elements of e-Games

Core Theoretical Framework

The 7 fundamental structural elements of e-Games from Topic 1 applied to Ready, Frame, Shoot!:

1. Rules

The governing constraints that structure player actions and world behavior.

  • Camera Settings: Interlocking exposure triangle rules (ISO, Aperture, Shutter Speed values affect lighting, depth of field, motion blur, and chromatic aberration).
  • Area Restrictions: Playable terrain boundaries restricting access to designated landscape zones.
  • Scoring Rules: High photo scores require matching specified camera parameters, accurate framing within focus zones, and correct viewing distance.

2. Goals

The motivational objectives that provide purpose and forward momentum.

  • Main Objective / Win Condition: Capture and document outdoor sceneries under requested lighting and composition conditions.
  • Optional Quests: Complete NPC side requests (e.g., taking requested photos for characters across the map).
  • Creative Sandbox: Capture non-objective scenic shots freely to save in the player's personal gallery.

3. Interaction

The communication loop between the player and the game world.

  • Navigation: First-person avatar navigation through 3D terrain.
  • Camera Operation: Aiming, framing, adjusting ISO/Aperture/Shutter Speed dials, zooming, and snapping photos.
  • Equipment Switching: Changing lenses and camera attachments.
  • NPC Interaction: Initiating dialogues to receive quest instructions.

4. Challenges

Obstacles that require player skill, knowledge, and problem-solving to overcome.

  • Learning Curve Scaffolding: Initial objectives are handheld with exact setting guidance; subsequent challenges require independent analysis of lighting and subject movement.
  • Environmental Composition: Finding optimal angles, framing moving subjects without unwanted blur, and avoiding out-of-focus capture.

5. Feedback

Sensory and emotional cues informing the player about action outcomes.

  • Emotional Feedback: Satisfaction of accomplishment tracked via an on-screen objective completion progress tracker.
  • Physical / Sensory Feedback: Camera shutter click sound when snapping, footstep audio on grass and dirt paths, and speech sound effects when talking to NPCs.
  • Visual Feedback: Real-time viewport preview of depth of field, exposure brightness, and motion blur.

6. Outcomes

The quantifiable consequences of player actions.

  • Photo Grading (Win Condition): Photos are calculated and graded based on player position, camera settings (ISO, Aperture, Shutter Speed), angle, framing, timing, and distance.
  • Non-Objective Capture: Photos out of focus zones or not tied to active objectives receive no grade, allowing players to freely keep screenshots as scenic mementos.

7. Representation

The expressive audio-visual and conceptual framing of the virtual world.

Visual Aesthetics

Photorealistic outdoor terrain with trees, grass, and flowers built with Unity native terrain tools, contrasted with clean low-poly character models and realistic FPS viewmodels.

Acoustic Design

Nature soundscape featuring birds chirping, bugs buzzing, and wind blowing, alongside tactile mechanical shutter clicks and NPC vocalizations.

Post-Processing

Unity Universal Render Pipeline (URP) post-processing stack simulating real camera optical effects (depth of field, exposure, motion blur, chromatic aberration).
