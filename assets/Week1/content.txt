Game Title : Ready, Frame, Shoot!
Game Genre: Educational
Game Objective : 
To teach basic photography composition techniques using a game.
Game Mechanics (3~4 features) : 
Camera view adjustment. Freely adjusting the game’s lighting setting via controlling a camera (ISO, Aperture, Shutter Speed), to snap photos of the landscape in a desired condition. (Main objective / win condition for the game)
Objective: To capture/document outdoor sceneries.
Challenge: First few objectives are handheld with tips to exact setting number, further down its up to the player to capture the perfect photo.
Representation. The player walks in a photorealistic outdoor terrain filled with trees; grass, and flowers built using the Unity native terrain tools. The ambience consists of nature sounds like birds chirping; bugs buzzing; wind blowing.
Rules: Camera settings, Area restrictions, Rules for scoring high on a photo.
Feedback. 
Emotional: Satisfaction of accomplishment. (Can be enforced by having a progress tracker of objectives completed)
Physical: Audio feedback when snapping a photo, footstep noise when walking on the grass/dirt path. NPC speech sound effects when spoken to.
Interaction : The player navigates the area and uses the camera tool to capture photos. The player can also interact with the camera tool to adjust camera settings or change camera equipment.

NPC Dialogues/Side Quests. NPCs around the map provide side quest objectives to be completed by taking photos of their desired locations/objects in specific camera settings. (Optional objective to be completed)
Example: Low-poly NPC character called Andrew sitting at a shack asking you to take a photo of a pond for him. 
Captured photos will be graded and calculated based on the player’s position, camera settings, angle, to determine their score. This is the winning condition of the game. (System)
Non objective photos (or photos out of focus zones) will not be graded. Players are allowed to freely capture scenic photos and keep the screenshots as they’d like.
Photos are graded based on camera settings (ISO, Aperture, Shutterspeed) (Optionally: framing; timing; distance)
