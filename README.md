1. Introduction
This is a group work, between 3-4 people in a group. The duration is from Week 1 to Week 4. It contributed 25% of coursework assessment. The learning outcome being assessed:
CLO2: Construct computer games with appropriate interaction models, control schemes and flow design with different game tuning parameters and techniques (P4, PLO3).
2. Your Assignment
Imagine that your group is a small game design studio. Your task is to design ONE original 3D game and show how your design grows from the first idea to a refined plan. Use the title “The Making of [Game Title]”. You may choose one theme from these themes, first come first serve. 
Culture Game
Educational Game

This is NOT a conventional written report. Create a digital website portfolio and update it every week (treat it as your game development journal). Use sketches, photos of working drafts, video, reading resources, diagrams, tables, short captions and before/after comparisons to SHOW what your group designed, HOW you designed it and WHY it changed. The same game will be implemented separately in the 3D Game Development course. Game programming, a Unity project, a playable build and a separate PDF report are NOT required for Assignment 1. 
3. Your Digital Platform
Each group must create one published digital website from Week 1 and maintain it through Week 4. Recommended using Google Sites. Any other digital platform such as Wix or another suitable free website platform is also allowed, provided the lecturer can open all pages using the submitted URL. The presentation of the digital portfolio should be readable and organized. Any website decoration and artistic drawing quality are not graded.
4. Weekly Tasks
Complete the main design tasks during practical session. Upload clear evidence and short design notes to your website each week. The tasks below are requirements, not separate reports.

WEEK 1 : The Birth of Our Game
Focus on Theme Research, Inspiration, Idea Exploration and Initial Game Concept
In Week 1, your group will explore the assigned theme, gather inspiration, discuss possible game ideas and develop your initial game concept. You are required to document your design process and weekly progress on your group's "The Making of [Game Title]" website. Your website should show how your ideas begin, develop and change throughout the design process, rather than showing only the final design.

Group Formation and Website Setup
Form a group of 3–4 members. Confirm your assigned theme: either Cultural Game or Educational Game. Introduce your group members and assign roles and responsibilities to each group member. Every group must have a Group Leader.  Create your group's digital platform. Google Sites is recommended, but other suitable free website platforms are allowed.

The details of the content – refer to Practical 001
Apply the relevant concepts from Topic 1: Overview of Computer Game Design, especially the Structural Elements of e-Games, to your initial game concept.
Show how the structural elements such as rules, goals, interaction, challenges, feedback, outcomes and representation are reflected in your proposed game.
Use your Game Concept Card, sketches or short design examples to demonstrate these elements.

WEEK 2 :  How Our Game Works
Focus on Game Rules, Mechanics, Player Interaction, Control Schemes, User Interface (UI) and Game Feedback
In Week 2, your group will continue developing the game concept created in Week 1. This week, you will focus on how your game works and how players interact with it. You will design the game rules, gameplay mechanics, player controls, user interface and feedback. You must document your design work on your "The Making of [Game Title]" website. Use sketches, diagrams, tables, images and short explanations to show your design ideas. You are not required to develop or program the game for this assignment.

The details of the content – refer to Practical 002
Apply the relevant concepts from your lectures to explain how your game works. Your design should demonstrate how appropriate concepts such as Operant Conditioning, Rewards and Penalties, Narrative, Puzzle Design, Feedback Design, Control Schemes, Items and Inventory, and UI Design are used in your game.
Show their application through your gameplay mechanics, interaction examples, control mapping, UI mockups and other relevant designs.

WEEK 3 :  Designing the Gameplay
Focus on Level Design, Game Flow, Challenges, Game Tuning and Asset Planning
In Week 3, your group will design the gameplay experience based on the game concept and mechanics developed in Weeks 1 and 2. You will plan the game levels, gameplay flow, challenges, difficulty and resources needed for your game. Document your design process using sketches, diagrams, tables and short explanations on your "The Making of [Game Title]" website.

The details of the content – refer to Practical 003
Apply the relevant concepts of Spatial Design, Game Flow, Level Design and Game Balancing to your gameplay design.
Show how these concepts influence your level layout, player navigation, challenge progression, difficulty and tuning parameters.
Use your game level maps, flowcharts and tuning tables to demonstrate your design decisions.

WEEK 4 :  Improving Our Game Design
Focus on Design Review, Improvements and Final Game Design
In Week 4, your group will review and improve the game design developed during Weeks 1–3. You must show the changes made to your design, explain the reasons for the improvements and complete your "The Making of [Game Title]" website for final submission.
